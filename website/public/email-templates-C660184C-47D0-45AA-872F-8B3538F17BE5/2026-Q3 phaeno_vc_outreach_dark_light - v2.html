I’m reaching out to introduce Phaeno and PSeq, our whole-molecule RNA-sequencing platform designed to deliver end-to-end, isoform-resolved transcript information using standard short-read NGS instruments.

Platform proposition
Whole-molecule RNA information delivered through broadly deployed short-read infrastructure

Conventional RNA-seq fragments transcripts and relies heavily on statistical reconstruction of the molecules present in a sample. PSeq takes a different approach: it preserves source-molecule identity across randomly generated fragments and uses that identity to reconstruct phased consensus sequences of individual RNA molecules.

This creates a richer and more traceable layer of biological information while retaining the scalability, economics, accuracy, and installed base of short-read sequencing.

New website and technical series
We recently launched a new Phaeno website and published four white papers describing the platform from source-molecule tagging and library construction through transcript reconstruction and raw-read-to-consensus evidence traceability.

The four papers are:

  • PSeq: Whole-Molecule Phased RNA Sequencing on Standard Short-Read NGS
  • PSeq Molecular Tagging and Library Architecture
  • PSeq Data Pipeline
  • Initial Technical Validation of the PSeq Whole-Molecule RNA Sequencing Platform

Together, the papers describe the platform architecture from source-molecule tagging and library construction through SMID-based read organization, transcript reconstruction, and raw-read-to-consensus data traceability.

We believe PSeq establishes a differentiated category between conventional short-read RNA-seq and third-generation long-read sequencing: whole-molecule RNA information delivered through broadly deployed short-read infrastructure.
