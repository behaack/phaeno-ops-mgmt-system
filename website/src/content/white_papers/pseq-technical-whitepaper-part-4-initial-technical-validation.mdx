## Abstract

PSeq combines source-molecule tagging, short-read library construction, SMID-based read clustering, transcript assembly, and data pipeline constructed  run-specific SQL evidence record. This paper consolidates initial technical evidence for PSeq v1 across that complete chain. The evidence confirms targeted library size-distribution, and key structural properties of library inserts: positionally labeled markers with barcodes, strand discriminators, random-fragment of source molecule, strands sense specified cDNA fragments.

Database assembled records of the sequencing of a representative (PRPF31 gene) transcript, visualized with the open source IGV gene viewer.  Records conform to population-level properties expected for the PSeq DNA library. These include barcodes linked to single transcripts of single source molecules from an identified source gene. Also, the absence of chimeras, with end-to-end high depth overlapping read coverage

The evidence separately conforms to correct automated computational assembly of single, phased, end-to-end consensus transcript sequences, preserving linked records from raw data FASTQ files through assembly steps and final structure, for each molecule, at scale. 

The observations support the successful integration of sample chemistry, conventional NGS on unmodified instruments and computational assembly of full-length transcript populations. They further indicate how whole-molecule alignment records can be interrogated. They should be interpreted as initial internal technical evidence, not as a statistically complete platform validation, clinical validation, or regulatory performance study.
