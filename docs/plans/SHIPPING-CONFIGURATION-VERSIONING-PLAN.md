## Product need and scope

Phaeno configuration administrators need to prepare content and relationship changes to **Sample types**, **Phaeno ship-to destinations**, **Shipping procedures**, and **Kit specifications** without changing instructions for new work until the change is ready. They need to see which revision is usable, edit or abandon an unfinished successor, activate a validated revision, and read exact history. Customer Orders, prepared kits, issued packets, shipments, and scans must retain their saved facts.

The owner wants the controlled Lab step, Protocol, and Workflow versioning pattern for these four records, with one explicit exception: an authorized Phaeno administrator can activate a validated shipping revision directly; no independent author/approver step is required. This does not waive existing configuration permissions, domain validation, dependency checks, audit, concurrency, or physical/scientific approval for referenced Lab workflows.

Success means an administrator can prepare one successor while the current revision remains usable, revise that draft in place without consuming another revision number, review its effects, and activate or discard it. New work resolves the intended revision; historical and issued records remain exact.

## Product decisions recorded

- An authorized Phaeno administrator may activate a validated shipping Draft directly. A second-person approval is not required.
- Staff may save an incomplete Draft and finish it over several edits. Activation requires complete validation.
- A Kit specification may become Active while its selected Sample type is still Draft or inactive and while a Phaeno assembly workflow is pending. These are independently released configurations. New Orders require an Active, effective Sample type and its Active Shipping procedure, plus an orderable kit product and specification. A missing assembly workflow warns without blocking specification activation; an approved but incompatible workflow still fails activation validation. Missing or incompatible current workflows warn during kit ordering. New physical Phaeno kits cannot be prepared until a compatible approved workflow exists; dispatch still requires a fully assembled or received, tube-verified physical kit.
- A relationship **between these four shipping entities** is part of the owning record's versioned content. Changing the Sample type's selected Shipping procedure requires a new Sample type Draft and activation. Changing a Kit specification's selected Sample type requires a new Kit specification Draft and activation. The previous immediate **Change procedure** action and permanent kit-to-Sample-type link are superseded by this decision. A Draft's relationships remain editable until activation; released revisions retain their original links.
- A newer approved version of the upstream kit assembly workflow does not, by itself, require a new Kit specification version. Assembly-workflow approval remains its own Lab control; compatibility with the current shipping specification and exact physical-kit lineage remain required.
- A placed/accepted Job keeps the exact Sample type and Shipping procedure revisions under which it began. Later activation of either family serves newly placed Jobs, not that Job's later unissued packets. Unplaced drafts must pass current-readiness validation when placed. Issued packets remain frozen.
- Ordinary deactivation of a Sample type or Shipping procedure stops new Jobs from selecting it. A Job already placed under that revision may continue to receive later packets using its exact pins. Stopping further use for an affected placed Job requires a separate explicit, audited safety hold; deactivation or supersession alone does not impose that hold. A held Job cannot issue another packet until the hold is explicitly resolved. Previously issued packets remain frozen.
- Once a released revision is displaced or deactivated, it cannot be reactivated. Restoring availability requires a new Draft revision and activation. A Draft may be edited until activation; **Discard** removes it from pending work while retaining its audit history and version number. Numbers are never reused.
- Newly approved kit assembly workflow revisions apply automatically to future assemblies when compatible with the Active Kit specification. Each physical kit keeps the exact workflow and component facts used to make it; a newer workflow alone does not revise the specification.

The current application has no placed-Job Sample type change action. The safety-hold design must therefore provide an explicit audited resolution path rather than silently changing a Job's pinned revisions. The local migration was separately authorized and applied after verification; the same approval does not authorize any production migration or historical Job repair.

## Reference model: what the Lab records actually share

- A stable identity owns numbered versions. Candidate content can be edited only while Draft; released content and exact references remain readable. Creating another candidate is blocked while an open candidate exists. Authorization, audit, optimistic concurrency, and a transition action protect release.
- Lab steps create identity plus an unconfigured Draft v1. Approved Lab step versions remain available for explicit selection into a Protocol. A Protocol embeds the selected step content and preserves the exact version reference.
- Protocols create the identity separately. Approval retires earlier Approved or Active Protocol versions. A service Workflow pins exact Protocol versions and has its own Draft → Approved → Production path; promoting it retires the preceding Production version. Kit assembly workflows instead use Draft → Approved → Retired. These are related controls, not one interchangeable status machine.
- The scientific author/approver separation and approval override belong to Lab governance. Shipping configuration will use Draft → direct activation by an authorized administrator, with record-specific readiness checks.

It currently defaults a newly created content revision to Active when permitted and treats relationship changes as separate, non-versioned actions. The owner's new Draft-first and versioned-relationship decisions supersede those two rules **for these four records only**. The owning shipping plans also contain the prior immediate procedure-change and permanent kit-link rules; update them with the implementation. Other numbered records are outside this change.

## Relationship boundary

| Relationship | Current model | Planned versioning rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sample type → Shipping procedure | One selected procedure family, changed by an immediate action that rewrites the procedure ID on all Sample type revisions. | The selected procedure **family** belongs to each Sample type revision. To select a different procedure family, create or edit the next Sample type Draft and activate it. A new revision within the same procedure family does not itself create a Sample type revision. A placed Job retains the exact procedure revision selected for it before that later activation. |
| Kit specification → Sample type | Zero or one Sample type family is linked once to the stable kit identity and cannot be reassigned. | The selected Sample type **family** belongs to each Kit specification revision. A successor may choose another family; the prior specification and physical kits keep their original Sample type. A new revision within the same Sample type family does not itself create a Kit specification revision. |
| Ship-to → another of these four records | No direct link exists in the current replacement shipping model. | Do not invent one. If a direct link is added later, it follows the same owning-revision rule. The global Default destination and a Job's saved destination are separate relationships outside these four entities. |
| Kit specification → kit assembly workflow | The specification pins one exact approved workflow version and copies its component list when activated. | The workflow is upstream Lab configuration, outside the four-entity relationship rule. A later approved workflow version alone does not create a Kit specification revision or change an existing physical kit. Future assemblies automatically use the current approved workflow after compatibility validation against the still-active specification. |

## Proposed common lifecycle

1. Preserve the existing family identifiers, numbered revision IDs, codes/SKUs, and historical links. A new identity gets Draft v1; an existing family can have at most one open Draft successor. Creating a successor copies the current released content, including its relationships to the other three shipping entities. Staff may edit those relationships on the Draft before activation. Keep product/SKU identity and other external relationships under their own domain rules.
2. Save and edit the same Draft revision repeatedly with an optimistic version check. A save changes neither its revision number nor the current usable revision. Missing required details are allowed in Draft; activation validates all required details and current dependencies. Draft saves still reject malformed data and invalid identifiers.
3. **Discard** an unwanted Draft through a named, audited action. Retain its number and history; a later candidate gets the next number and does not reuse a discarded number. The next draft copies the latest released content when there is one.
4. **Activate** the latest complete Draft directly after server validation. For Sample types, destinations, and kit specifications, respect effective dates and keep the predecessor usable until the successor takes effect. Procedure activation takes effect immediately because procedures have no scheduling model. Do not silently advance issued snapshots or exact saved references.
5. Treat status and timing separately: Draft, released, explicitly deactivated, and discarded are persisted lifecycle facts; Active now, Scheduled, and Ended are derived from the effective interval where one exists. A displaced or deactivated released revision cannot be reactivated, even if no successor exists. An Active label alone does not prove an order, destination, or physical kit is ready for new work. A placed Job continues with its pinned revisions after routine supersession or ordinary deactivation. A separate explicit safety hold blocks its later packet issuance until resolved; never infer a safety stop from supersession or deactivation alone.
6. Show the currently usable revision on the family list, with an exact link to a newer Draft. The Draft detail exposes Edit, Activate, and Discard in one Actions menu; historical details remain view-first with revision history after the record and relationships. Name exact revision numbers and changed relationships in confirmations and impact warnings. Remove the immediate **Change procedure** and one-time **Link Sample type** actions when their replacement Draft flows ship.
7. Enforce one Draft and serialized transitions at the family boundary in the API and database, not only in the UI. Keep backend authorization, current audit stamping, version checks, and exact-revision snapshot rules. Neither superseded nor deactivated released revisions can be reactivated.

## Record-specific release checks and relationships

- **Sample type:** validate scientific/material requirements and the procedure family selected on this exact revision before activation. A missing usable kit may continue to make the type unorderable without changing the type's saved lifecycle state. Preserve the one-type-per-Order rule and issued instruction snapshots. Changing procedure family is part of the next Sample type Draft, never a mutation of earlier revisions.
- **Ship-to:** validate address, receiving, effective interval, and the global-default impact. The default remains a family choice; already-started Jobs retain their exact destination. Publishing a new destination may require explicit downstream configuration before new work is ready; surface that impact before activation.
- **Shipping procedure:** validate all shared instructions before activation. Resolve the exact Active procedure revision when a Job is placed and retain that revision for its later unissued packets, even after routine supersession or ordinary deactivation. Newly placed Jobs use the newer Active revision; ordinary deactivation blocks new selection. An explicit safety hold blocks later packets for affected placed Jobs until resolved. Already-issued packet text remains unchanged.
- **Kit specification:** keep the finished-product identity/SKU on the stable kit identity, but move the Sample type relationship to each specification revision. A successor may change its selected Sample type; active-specification, recommendation, and stock queries must use the selected family on the exact specification revision. Earlier physical kits remain attributable to their original specification and Sample type. An old historical kit may be Active for record management yet fail the separate new-order readiness gate.
- A Kit specification Draft may select a Sample type family whose only available revision is Draft, so both configurations can be prepared together. The picker shows one choice per family: the currently Active revision when one exists, otherwise the open Draft. It labels Draft status. The Sample type choice may remain blank while saving a Kit specification Draft, but activation requires a selected existing Sample type family. The Kit specification can become Active while that family is still Draft or otherwise inactive; new-work readiness requires an Active, effective Sample type revision and its Active Shipping procedure.
- The finished kit product supplies the fixed SKU and an initial suggested name. The Kit specification name is visible and editable on its Draft; the submitted name persists through creation and Draft edits, and a successor may rename its specification without changing the product identity. Keep the effective-date labels close to their controls.
- September 26 extension: the first field is the editable Kit specification name, followed by Supplier then Product. The Supplier list includes active suppliers with at least one active Transportation kit product; changing Supplier clears Product. Product identity, rather than SKU text alone, distinguishes equal product numbers across suppliers. Phaeno-made products retain workflow approval and assembly; an external supplier product uses a recorded purchased-kit receipt and verified physical tube roster. See the [Transportation kit product plan](TRANSPORTATION-KIT-PRODUCT-AND-BOM-PLAN.md#september-26-authorized-extension-purchased-complete-kits) for the receipt contract and distinct physical lineage.
- September 26 owner revision: the same catalog product and SKU may back multiple independently versioned Kit specification families, including concurrently Active families. Keep the one-open-Draft rule within each family, and resolve recommendations, physical kits, and issued history by exact specification revision ID. Selecting a SKU already present in any other specification family, including inactive or historical records, shows a nonblocking warning with its status, name, and configuration context; it does not prevent Draft creation or activation. Staff create a revision when replacing a family and a separate family when configurations coexist.
- Kit specification details use the canonical `/sample-shipping-settings/kit-specifications/:containerId` route and the shared Samples & shipping settings page layout, including its header and sidebar with Kit specifications selected. Their **Back to kit specifications** link returns to the all-kit list with its search, availability, inactive toggle, and page preserved. Sample type, destination, procedure, and kit detail back links share the arrow, alignment, and spacing pattern. The Sample type name remains a separate relationship link. Detail cards span the workspace width, matching Revision history.
- **Upstream kit assembly:** approval and revisions stay in the Lab workflow model. Do not mutate an activated Kit specification's content or copied component snapshot merely because a newer workflow is approved. Treat any specification-time workflow pin and copied components as historical release evidence, not the live bill of materials for every later assembly; a specification activated before approval has no such release snapshot. Resolve the latest approved workflow when preparing each new physical kit, recheck that its tube count and components satisfy the fixed specification, and pin the exact workflow and actual component facts on that physical kit. Workflow absence or mismatch blocks new assembly, while the Active specification remains orderable with a visible fulfillment warning. Existing physical kits remain exact.

## Data transition and implementation sequence

`IsActive = false` does not identify whether an existing Sample type, destination, or procedure was never activated, routinely superseded, or explicitly deactivated. Audit existing rows and transition history on a verified database copy before backfill. Preserve ambiguous records as legacy Inactive for review rather than guessing Draft or Deactivated. Backfill each historical Sample type revision's selected procedure and each Kit specification revision's selected Sample type from verified existing links and audit evidence, flagging ambiguous cases rather than silently assigning a current link to past content. Existing Jobs already store an exact Sample type revision ID, but packet issuance currently advances to the latest Active family revision and procedure. Record an exact procedure revision when a Job is placed; for already-placed Jobs, derive a pin only from trustworthy saved evidence and put unresolved cases into explicit review. Do not rewrite existing IDs, numbered references, issued packet JSON, kit snapshots, or shipment history.

1. Inventory existing revision, relationship, Job, packet, and kit evidence on a verified database copy. Classify ambiguous Inactive rows and already-placed Jobs for review; do not infer an original procedure pin from today's Active revision. Define the separate audited safety-hold and resolution contract without changing ordinary deactivation into a hold.
2. Design the additive lifecycle/audit migration and exact API contract. Retain the existing identity keys and kit type rather than replacing historical references. Store procedure choice on each Sample type revision, Sample type choice on each Kit specification revision, and the exact procedure revision pinned by a placed Job; retire the family-wide mutation paths after a verified backfill. Keep the upstream workflow's own revisions separate. Add a database-enforced one-Draft-per-family constraint and family-level serialization. Update `docs/database-erd.md` with any persisted-model change. Do not apply a migration to a shared database without explicit authorization.
3. Implement API create, draft update, activate, Discard, and release-history reads one record type at a time. Keep the existing resolver and snapshots correct at each slice. Make direct activation recheck current dependencies at the requested effective time. At Job placement, verify current readiness and save the exact Sample type and procedure revisions; packet creation thereafter uses those pins unless an explicit safety hold blocks issuance.
4. Adapt list, detail, revision form, impact preview, Actions, focus restoration, narrow layout, and Phaeno help. Reconcile the general revision strategy and owning shipping plans with the approved lifecycle.
5. At an authorized verification checkpoint, cover one-open-draft concurrency, repeated draft edits without renumbering, Discard and next number, scheduled replacement, exact historical links, relationship inheritance and reassignment by successor, placed-Job pins through routine supersession and ordinary deactivation, explicit safety-hold blocking and resolution, upstream workflow adoption/compatibility, and unchanged issued packets/physical kits. Manual signed-in acceptance and physical validation remain separate from synthetic tests.

The API/domain changes belong in the existing Order Management shipping controllers, `SampleShippingContainerCatalogService`, and their domain records. The current packet and readiness resolvers (`SampleShippingRevisionData`, destination choices, kit readiness) are the critical continuity paths. The existing Samples & shipping configuration panels and exact-revision detail pages own the UI change. Implementation must update the Phaeno configuration guide and any affected Customer/Partner shipping help, plus the backend, frontend, and E2E living test plans. The general [revision strategy](../revision-strategy.md) and the [shipping restart plan](SAMPLE-SHIPPING-RESTART-PLAN.md) need their prior relationship rules superseded explicitly for this scope when the feature ships.

## Acceptance scenarios for the later implementation

| Scenario | Expected product behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Create one of the four records | Draft v1 is saved even if required release details are incomplete. It is visible to an administrator and unavailable for new work. The UI identifies missing activation requirements. |
| Revise a released record | A single Draft successor inherits its content and its relationships to the other shipping entities. The currently usable revision remains the list's primary record and continues serving new work until replacement takes effect. A second Draft is blocked with a link to the existing one. |
| Continue drafting | Repeated saves change the same Draft without increasing its revision number. A stale save gets a conflict that preserves the editor's entered data for review. Released content cannot be edited. |
| Activate a Draft | An authorized Phaeno administrator sees the exact revision and downstream effect, then activates without a second-person approval. The server rechecks completeness, effective dates, current relationships and dependencies, and the expected concurrency version. An invalid Draft stays editable. |
| Replace an active revision | The predecessor remains usable until a scheduled successor takes effect where scheduling exists. An ended or displaced predecessor never becomes current again by accident. Existing exact references and issued snapshots remain readable. |
| Discard a Draft | The pending successor gate clears, but the Discarded entry, actor, time, and revision number remain in history. The next Draft uses the next number. No released revision is removed through this action. |
| Inspect list and history | Status, values, preview, and primary link describe the same usable revision; a newer Draft has its own exact link. Its next unissued packet still uses those exact pinned revisions; newly placed Jobs use only currently usable revisions. A separate explicit safety hold blocks later packet issuance until resolved. Already-issued packet snapshots remain frozen.
