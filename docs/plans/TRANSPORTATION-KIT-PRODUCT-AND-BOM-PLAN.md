## Product discovery and decision

**Users:** Phaeno configuration administrators approve kit products and shipping specifications; Phaeno kit staff assemble and dispatch individual kits; Customer staff acknowledge delivered kits and assign them to Jobs; shipping and receiving staff use the resulting frozen packing and barcode records.

**Problem:** **Container sizes** suggests that capacity alone defines a kit. The current record also carries a SKU, sample and destination compatibility, packing instructions, and a component list. The supplier catalog separately names the tube and outer shipper products. During preparation, staff select the actual tube and shipper again, so the configured contents are guidance rather than an authoritative bill of materials (BOM). This duplicates identity and permits a prepared kit to differ from its approved contents without an explicit substitution record.

**Owner decision:** For the first release, Phaeno assembles finished transportation kits from purchased components. A Phaeno transportation kit is a named product with an approved shipping specification and a versioned assembly workflow whose structured inputs form its BOM. Use the existing Lab Steps, Protocols, and Workflows construct for the manufacturing procedure. Phaeno may eventually purchase complete, ready-to-use transportation kits from external suppliers, but external finished-kit receipt is deferred. This expands the earlier Phaeno catalog rule, which currently permits only Reagent products, to include Transportation kit products. Reagent and transportation-kit production remain distinct workflows.

**Outcome:** A staff member selects a finished transportation kit product and its approved revision, sees exactly which components and tubes belong in one kit, records the physical assembly, and obtains one unique container barcode. Shipping recommendations use the product revision's usable capacity and approved sample, destination, and handling combinations. Inventory remains a count and history of individual physical kits, not a count of product definitions.

## Product language and identity

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Transportation kit product | The finished, named Phaeno product offered for sample transport. It has one stable catalog identity and SKU across revisions. |
| Shipping specification | An approved revision of that product's usable tube capacity, eligible sample and destination assignments, temperature control, packing steps, and effective period. |
| Kit assembly workflow | The approved, ordered Lab steps/protocols for making one physical kit, linked to the finished product. A run records step execution and actual component use without inventing a sample or sample tube. |
| Bill of materials | Structured required inputs on an approved kit assembly workflow revision: the exact catalog products and positive whole-number quantities needed for one finished kit. It identifies the supplied tube product and count, outer shipper, and any other included products. |
| Shipping container component | The purchased outer shipper used by a kit. The existing **Shipping Container** product type describes this component; it is not the finished transportation kit product. |
| Physical kit | One assembled container and its registered permanent tube barcodes, identified by its unique `KIT-` barcode and tracked through stock, dispatch, receipt, assignment, and use. |

The product SKU identifies the kind of finished kit; the `KIT-` barcode identifies one physical instance. Do not add a separate finished-kit lot number. Retain supplier lot and expiration facts for components when applicable; those identify purchased batches, not the finished kit. Do not substitute a product SKU, a supplier product number, or a tube barcode for the physical kit identity.

The administrator enters the SKU when creating the Phaeno transportation-kit product. It is read-only thereafter, and the API rejects a changed SKU even before a shipping specification exists. The **Kit name** can be corrected until a shipping specification is linked; then the name is fixed as well. Product inventory units offer a common-unit picker while retaining free-text entry for valid units outside the list. A finished kit's unit remains `each` and read-only.

## First-release workflow

1. In **Suppliers & products**, an authorized administrator creates a Phaeno **Transportation kit** product with a stable name and SKU. The built-in Phaeno supplier remains visibly flagged as an internal producer. The existing **Shipping Container** type remains available for purchased outer shippers; Reagent remains its own product type.
2. In the product's shipping specification, the administrator sets usable tube capacity and selects the exact approved sample-type, destination, and handling assignments. Each applicable combination retains its temperature-control and packing instructions. Approval/effective dates continue to determine the current revision.
3. In **Lab settings → Workflows**, the administrator configures one kit assembly workflow for the finished product. Its revision orders reusable approved Lab steps/protocols and owns the structured BOM. Preserve the existing ability to include any number of distinct component products across suppliers, each with an independent positive quantity. Designate one tube catalog product for the included tube roster, with its quantity matching the number of tubes supplied in one kit. Record the purchased outer shipper and other supplies as BOM inputs. Validate component product type, supplier/product status, quantities, and duplicate lines before approval. A different tube type or component quantity requires a reviewed workflow revision or an explicit, auditable substitution path; ordinary assembly cannot silently override the BOM.
4. Approve a usable product release only when its shipping specification and exact kit assembly workflow/BOM revision agree on the tube product, count, usable capacity, and effective period. A draft or mismatched pair is not selectable for new assembly or shipping. The released product points to both exact revisions so one cannot change underneath the other.
5. In **Lab operations → Transportation kits**, staff choose the current approved Phaeno kit product to assemble. Show the pinned workflow steps and BOM as the procedure and pick list. Record step completion and actual component products; when the underlying component is lot-tracked or expirable, record its source lot and expiration evidence. For inventory-tracked components, record use against available inventory once as assembly happens, with actor and time. A stopped assembly retains recorded use and requires an explained correction for unused or returned components; it must not invent finished stock.
6. Scan the permanent identifier on every actual tube as it is placed in the physical kit, using the selected tube product to establish its manufacturer namespace. Allocate the physical kit's unique barcode. Review the complete saved tube roster against the packed contents before marking the kit ready. Keep the exact product release, shipping specification, assembly workflow/BOM revisions, actual components, step evidence, tube identities, source evidence, and instructions on the kit record or immutable snapshot.
7. Existing Customer requests, dispatch, receipt, location ownership, reservation, Job assignment, tube matching, and sample accession continue against the individual physical kit. Recommendation and eligibility still require an active, effective, compatible shipping specification and the existing stock/receipt safeguards. A later product or workflow revision does not silently change a prepared kit, issued packet, or shipment.

The product's catalog unit is **each**. Component inventory uses each component product's own saved unit. Do not derive a component use quantity from the kit's tube capacity when the BOM specifies a different quantity; reject an inconsistent revision before approval.

## Reuse of Lab Steps, Protocols, and Workflows

Reuse the existing controlled authoring and approval pattern: reusable Lab steps describe bench actions and evidence, protocols group those steps, and the kit assembly workflow sequences exact approved versions for one finished kit product. At any time a product has at most one current approved kit assembly workflow; revisions preserve its history. A started assembly pins its exact workflow/protocol/step versions and BOM. Product identity remains the finished kit's name and SKU, not the workflow name.

The current Lab protocol resource captures are scoped to preparation batches and sample tubes; the current reagent workflow has sample-independent ordered steps but no structured required-product quantities. Extend the shared constructs with a **physical kit** execution scope, product/BOM inputs, component-use evidence, tube-barcode registration, and completion checks. Do not route kit assembly through library preparation or create placeholder customer samples, sample tubes, or reagent lots. Keep the BOM in one structured, queryable workflow revision; step prose may explain how to use a component but must not be the sole source of its identity or quantity. The shipping specification owns sample compatibility and temperature/packing instructions for the Customer shipment, while the assembly workflow owns how Phaeno makes the finished kit.

## Physical tube identity is a release gate

- The BOM specifies the tube catalog product and required count. It never creates tube IDs. Every tube ID comes from scanning the permanent identifier on the actual tube being packed. Store a stable physical-tube record with the normalized printed value, manufacturer namespace, selected product, and exact physical `KIT-` container; show the readable value and the completed count to the operator.
- For one manufacturer namespace, one printed tube value can belong to only one physical tube/kit. Reject duplicates within a scan batch, existing stock kits, registered return kits, and accessioned Lab containers. Different manufacturers may print the same value, so compare namespace plus value and treat unresolved legacy values conservatively. Serialize concurrent registrations and make retries unable to add a tube twice.
- Keep an assembling kit unavailable until the number of distinct saved tube identities equals the approved tube count, the recorded tube product matches the pinned BOM, and an operator verifies the physical roster before sealing. A barcode count alone does not prove the correct tubes were packed. The final review shows each readable tube ID with the kit barcode and captures who verified it and when. Dispatch and Customer availability require this completion gate.
- A mistaken scan before dispatch has a controlled correction with actor, time, reason, and the replaced identity; the prior association remains in audit history. After dispatch, ordinary editing of the frozen tube roster is prohibited and a discrepancy uses an explicit exception/recovery path. Never silently move a registered tube to another kit or manufacture a placeholder ID to fill a count.
- When the Customer assigns a kit to a return shipment and matches samples, carry each exact physical tube identity forward through the return-kit roster, shipment manifest, scan crosswalk, and Lab accession. Retain a stable link or explicit one-to-one lineage from stock registration; copied barcode text alone is insufficient evidence that the right physical tube was used. A tube cannot be assigned to two active kits, two shipments, or two samples.

## Transportation kits workspace

Place **Transportation kits** directly after **Reagent manufacturing** in the Lab operations sidebar, before the divider leading to **Suppliers & products**. The workspace owns **Inventory** (default), **Kit requests**, and the existing shipment-specific **Kits sent** work. **Prepare kit** is an action from Inventory, not the name of the inventory itself. **Receipt & accession** then contains only **Receive shipments** and **Accession samples**.

Inventory shows searchable individual kit records and counts by finished product/SKU, status, and location. Distinguish ready at Phaeno, on the way, Customer-confirmed available, assigned, in use, and needs-review states. Unknown or unconfirmed stock must not be shown as verified available. Opening a kit shows its versioned product/BOM and physical history. Preserve current permissions and Customer/Department/location isolation. Update navigation from requests and delivery locations, return links, filters, URL-backed tabs, help, and older bookmarked `receiptTab` links without changing the underlying dispatch or receipt transaction.

The Inventory list defaults to **At Phaeno** (preparing and ready physical kits). Its **Shipped kits** filter includes every dispatched state, including in use and needs review, while individual statuses and **All statuses** remain available for investigation. Search, pagination, record links, and clear filters preserve or restore the selected list view through URL state. A shipped kit without a recorded delivery location remains outside the default Phaeno stock view even though it needs review.

Product configuration stays with its catalog identity. **Lab settings → Workflows** links the kit assembly procedure to that product. Samples & shipping settings may link to, preview, and approve its shipping specification; it must not create a second independently named “container size” product. Retain sample types, destinations, shared shipping procedures, and assignments as their existing controlled records.

## Acceptance and success measures

- An administrator can create a Phaeno transportation kit product, draft/revise its shipping specification and kit assembly workflow/BOM, inspect both histories, and approve only a valid, complete paired product release. Reagent products and purchased component products retain their own identities and rules.
- A product revision makes the supported sample/handling/destination combinations, usable capacity, exact tube product, and all BOM quantities clear. Preview and Customer recommendations exclude inactive, ineffective, or incompatible revisions and preserve the current capacity/handling safeguards.
- Staff cannot assemble a kit with an unapproved workflow or shipping specification, missing BOM components, a mismatched tube product or count, duplicate physical/tube barcodes, or unavailable required tracked stock. A run records its pinned step versions and completion evidence without inventing a Customer Job sample. Every recorded use is attributable to a source and cannot be deducted twice on retry. A permitted substitution has explicit approval and is visible on the physical kit.
- A kit with even one missing, duplicate, wrong-manufacturer, or unverified physical tube ID cannot become ready, be dispatched, or appear as Customer-available stock. Corrections preserve history, and the exact verified roster remains traceable through Customer tube matching, frozen manifests, and Lab accession.
- The inventory total for each status and location equals the count of its underlying physical kit records. Customer-available counts require confirmed receipt; dispatched, assigned, in-use, and needs-review kits are not counted as freely available Phaeno stock.
- Existing kit requests, physical kits, customer receipts, reservations, printed barcodes, and issued shipment/packing snapshots remain addressable and unchanged after transition. Older links return users to the equivalent new workspace context.
- Focused backend tests cover product/BOM validation, paired release and version pinning, step/protocol approval, sample-independent kit execution, revision immutability, sample compatibility, component use and retry/concurrency, physical kit identity, tube namespace collisions, partial/corrected rosters, stock-to-return-to-Lab lineage, and historical backfill. Frontend and E2E coverage covers catalog/configuration, workflow authoring, scan/review/roster correction, assembly, inventory, navigation, permissions, and the Customer request-to-receipt handoff. Physical printer/scanner and packing qualification remain separate operational acceptance.

Success means staff can name the finished product and its approved contents without reselecting an unrelated “container size,” can account for every assembled kit by barcode, and can identify which actual component products went into it. The interface must not present a count derived from unconfirmed Customer stock as an available balance.

## Deferred extension: externally purchased complete kits

Later work will define receipt of a complete purchased kit, supplier barcode adoption or Phaeno barcode assignment, acceptance/inspection, the supplier's included tube roster and component provenance, and how supplier changes affect approval. Do not add a purchased-finished-kit receipt path, infer its internal BOM consumption, or relabel purchased outer shippers as finished kits in the first release.
