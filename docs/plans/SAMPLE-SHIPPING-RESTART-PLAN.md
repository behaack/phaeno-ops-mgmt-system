# Samples and shipping restart plan

Status: implementation in progress September 25, 2026. Replacement domain, API, UI, and schema changes are being reconciled. The narrow replacement-schema migration has been applied to the configured local database after backup and clone verification. The broader local Order/sample/shipping data reset and the production reset have not started. The owner lifted the verification restriction; build, typecheck, and tests are permitted. Both broader resets require the concrete dependency inventory, backup evidence, and cutover manifest described below.

## Goal and scope

Replace the current Samples & shipping configuration with four understandable records: **Sample type**, **Phaeno ship-to destination**, **Transportation kit**, and **Shipping procedure**. Remove Shipping assignments, compatibility groups, destination-by-Sample-type rules, and other entities or fields that have no role in the new model. Do not retain legacy configuration merely to keep obsolete forms or APIs working.

The requested data restart covers local and production configuration, Lab Service Orders/Jobs, their samples, and sample/shipping records. The removal inventory must enumerate order-owned commercial, finance, laboratory, result, kit-request, stock, shipment, packet, and audit dependencies before execution. Delete dependent records that cannot exist without a removed Order; do not leave orphaned references or dangling external files. Shared Company, user, supplier/product, and unrelated records are outside this draft boundary unless the inventory proves they are exclusively owned by removed Orders. The exact inventory and counts are an execution gate, not a reason to preserve the old shipping model.

## Product contract

| Record | Relationship and availability |
| --- | --- |
| Sample type | Exactly one selected Shipping procedure. It may have many Transportation kits but needs at least one usable kit before ordering. It can be saved as a draft while kit setup is incomplete. One Sample type per Order. |
| Shipping procedure | May serve zero or many Sample types. An unavailable procedure strands linked Sample types for new work. |
| Transportation kit | Starts with zero or one Sample type. Once assigned to a Sample type, that link cannot be changed; all later kit revisions retain it. A Sample type revision does not break the link. Dry-ice quantity belongs in the kit specification. The kit's bill of materials remains a structured component list. |
| Phaeno ship-to destination | Related to Orders, not to Sample types, procedures, or kits. Exactly one available destination is designated Default whenever ordering is enabled. Missing or unavailable Default blocks ordering. |

At Order creation, capture one Sample type. An Order may have many physical kits, including quantities of different compatible Transportation kit designs; every kit must be linked to that Order's one Sample type. At fulfillment, staff choose eligible designs and physical kits; preselect the only eligible design where appropriate. Every dispatched physical kit retains its design, exact revision, and Order link. Assign the Order's destination at its first kit dispatch, then keep it fixed for later dispatches under that Order. If a customer uses an old kit, assign the then-current Default when the Order is submitted. Later Default or content changes must not silently redirect that Order or rewrite issued instructions.

Ordinary deactivation of a kit design stops new assembly and new kit requests for that design. A kit request already queued before deactivation may still be fulfilled from existing physical inventory that matches its Order's Sample type, is in date, and is not withdrawn or recalled. An already dispatched customer-held kit may likewise be used for a new Order if physically usable, in date, and not withdrawn or recalled; it does not become invalid merely because a newer content revision is Active. A separate withdrawal/recall action blocks affected physical kits from both queued fulfillment and customer use.

Every Active Phaeno destination can receive every orderable Sample type, subject to the destination's general receiving and carrier restrictions. There is no per-type destination approval matrix.

## Revisions and relationships

- A content revision changes versioned record content. It inherits the record's existing relationships. Relationship selectors do not appear in the Create revision form.
- A relationship change is a separate, audited action and does not increment the content revision number. Sample type Actions includes **Change procedure**. An unassigned Transportation kit can be linked once; no later reassignment action is offered.
- Add Sample type establishes its required procedure relationship. Add Transportation kit may save an unassigned draft. The reverse lists on Procedure and Sample type detail pages are views of those relationships, not competing edit paths.
- Active content and usable-for-ordering are different states. Before deactivating a dependency, identify affected records. Persist a red warning on each stranded record's list and detail view, and block its use for new work until repaired. Do not label an unusable record as ready merely because its own revision is Active.

## Interface and instruction flow

- Provide a list and dedicated, view-first detail page for each of the four records. A list row shows each relationship with at most one related record, including **None** where allowed. Detail pages show every relationship, including one-to-many collections, with links and warnings for unavailable records.
- Keep relationship actions in the item's **Actions** menu. Use bounded modals for Add, Create revision, and relationship changes; preserve list state and accessible focus behavior.
- Retain one current Active/effective content revision per record family for new work. New procedure revisions flow into new instructions through the Sample type's procedure relationship. Physical dispatched kits and issued packets retain the exact content and facts used.
- Present shared procedure instructions as read-only text on the relevant previews. The kit specification owns container-specific packing, temperature control, dry-ice amount, capacity, and bill of materials as applicable. Destination details own receiving and delivery information. Remove the former assignment-specific instruction layer.
- Make **Transportation kit** one configuration workspace for its named product/SKU, versioned shipping specification, dry-ice quantity, capacity, packing, and bill of materials, even if assembly uses existing controlled Lab workflows internally. Keep individual barcoded physical kits in operational inventory, linked to the exact approved design revision used to assemble them. Do not make users coordinate several independently named configuration records to understand one kit.
- Allow a Default destination switch as one action with one current Default. Warn before disabling the Default and require a valid replacement or leave ordering blocked; never silently choose a different destination.
- Update Phaeno, Customer, Partner, and any affected Prospect help only when the corresponding behavior is implemented. Update navigation, setup guidance, API contracts, ERD, and the living test plans together.

## Resolved product decisions and cutover boundaries

1. **Kits per Order.** An Order may have multiple physical kits, with quantities of different compatible designs. Every kit must match its single Sample type. Selection, partial dispatch, and exact-revision snapshots operate per request line and physical kit.
2. **Kit deactivation and queued work.** Ordinary deactivation stops new assembly and requests. Requests already queued may be fulfilled from existing in-date, non-recalled physical inventory; customer-held usable kits may support new Orders. Withdrawal/recall is distinct and blocks affected physical kits.
3. **Destination eligibility.** Every Active Phaeno destination can receive every orderable Sample type, subject to general receiving and carrier restrictions. There is no per-type destination assignment or approval matrix.
4. **Production data.** Production is a testing environment only, according to the owner. Its Orders, samples, sample/shipping records, and configuration may be discarded as a one-time test-data reset. This does not create a normal hard-delete workflow or alter the long-term retention policy for real records. Inventory, backup, file/external-reference handling, and an unexpected-real-data stop remain required.
5. **Deletion boundary.** This plan interprets Orders as Lab Service/PSeq Orders/Jobs, including Partner Lab Service use. The inventory must identify Trial shipments, Finance receipts and allocations, standalone physical stock, and unrelated Order classes touched by the purge, then show their explicit disposition before execution.
6. **Old kit identification.** A customer-held kit Order identifies each physical kit and confirms its locked Sample type, physical availability, and withdrawal/recall/expiry state before applying the Default destination. A product name alone is insufficient.
7. **Fixed destination after dispatch.** Once a Job has actually sent its first kit, later dispatches keep the exact saved Phaeno destination revision even if that revision is subsequently deactivated or superseded. This continuation never makes the destination selectable for a new Job and never redirects a committed route. Staff must explicitly confirm that receiving can accept the remaining kits before recording dispatch to a now-unavailable saved destination; the server enforces that confirmation. A missing saved destination remains blocked for repair.

## Acceptance cases

- New Sample type requires one procedure but can remain a draft without a kit. New work is blocked until its procedure and at least one linked kit are usable.
- Procedure can be used by zero, one, or many Sample types. Changing the selected procedure uses a separate audited action and does not create a Sample type revision.
- Kit may be unassigned during setup, then linked once to exactly one Sample type. A revision of either record preserves that link. Only compatible kits appear for an Order.
- An Order may request multiple compatible kit designs and quantities. With one eligible design, fulfillment preselects it; with several, staff choose. Each dispatched physical kit retains its choice and exact revision. Partial dispatches keep one fixed Order destination.
- Ordinary kit deactivation prevents new assembly and requests, but queued requests may use existing in-date, non-recalled inventory of the requested design. Already held usable kits may support new Orders; a withdrawal/recall blocks affected physical kits.
- Exactly one available Default destination is used when an old kit is submitted. For a newly sent kit, fulfillment assigns the destination at dispatch. Absence of a valid Default blocks ordering.
- Deactivating a dependency shows affected records before confirmation, leaves visible persistent warnings, and blocks new work that would rely on it.
- Lists show only zero-or-one relationships; detail pages show all relationships with working links and unavailable-state warnings.
- Local and production reset manifests account for every removed Order, sample, shipment, stock/kit request, packet, and dependent record. Remaining records have no dangling references. Obsolete assignment and compatibility tables, fields, APIs, UI, and help are gone.
- Every Active destination is available to every orderable Sample type without an assignment record; the destination's general receiving and carrier restrictions still apply.
